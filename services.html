AI that reviews your UX like a senior designer. The audit shortened the path to a first useful suggestion and gave teams a reason to come back.

What the research showed

The product page is a JavaScript app with no visible copy for crawlers or first-time visitors, and public listings give no tiers, limits or proof. That makes onboarding, a visible free-plan limit and a before and after example the highest-value places to improve.

Sources, checked Oct 2026: onBeaconAIPure listing. Figures marked self-reported come from the product's own site.

The challenge

onBeacon promises senior-designer feedback in minutes. That promise only holds if a new team reaches its first useful suggestion quickly, and then has a reason to return. The brief was to improve the existing features, tighten onboarding and build retention.

Approach

  1. Walk the activation path

    Mapped every step from landing to first suggestion and marked where a new user has to think, wait or guess.

  2. Review how feedback is delivered

    Checked how suggestions are worded, ranked and acted on, so a team knows what to fix first.

  3. Design the return loop

    Looked for moments after the first audit that give a team a reason to come back: re-scans, progress and change tracking.

  4. Prioritise

    Scored every finding by impact and effort and grouped them into quick wins and bigger bets.

Key findings
  • The first-run experience should show value on the user's own page before asking for anything else.
  • Suggestions need a visible priority, so teams can tell a critical fix from a nice-to-have.
  • Without progress tracking, a finished audit gives no reason to return.
  • Empty and loading states are the best place to teach what the product does.
Recommendations
  • Open onboarding with a sample audit of the user's own URL, so they see value in the first minute.
  • Add a short, progressive setup checklist that ends with the first fix marked as done.
  • Rank suggestions by impact and add one-click states such as fixed, later and not relevant.
  • Send a weekly re-scan digest showing the score trend and the next best fix.
Deliverables
  • Annotated screenshots of the key flows
  • Prioritised action plan (impact vs effort)
  • Written summary for the team
  • Follow-up session to answer questions

What the research showed

Codiris describes itself as an agentic Product Development Environment for the whole lifecycle, but the homepage shows only a prompt box and desktop-style controls. The gap between the big promise and the first screen is the core UX problem.

Sources, checked Oct 2026: CodirisHumiris launch post. Figures marked self-reported come from the product's own site.

The challenge

Codiris lets a visitor turn a website or an idea into an agent-first app. The idea is strong, but a first-time visitor has to work out what 'agent-first' means, what they will get and what to do next.

Approach

  1. First-visit review

    Read the page as a new visitor would, with no prior context, and noted every moment of hesitation.

  2. Language and jargon pass

    Listed the terms that are never defined and the labels that do not say what is behind them.

  3. Navigation and CTA check

    Reviewed how the related products, domains and calls to action guide a visitor forward.

  4. Trust and polish

    Checked the signals that tell a visitor the product is reliable, including the disclaimer and meta data.

Key findings
  • The outcome is unclear: 'Transform [site] into an Agent-first app' does not say what the user gets or how it works.
  • 'Agent-first' and 'agents' are never defined for a visitor who has not met the idea.
  • Related products (Agentizer, Agentnet, Iris) have no descriptions behind their names.
  • No primary call to action, sign-up or pricing is visible; the prompt box is the only way forward.
  • The 'Codiris can make mistakes' notice is small and easy to miss.
  • Several Codiris domains appear without context, which weakens orientation.
  • Empty or duplicated meta tags suggest the page may not preview cleanly when shared.
Recommendations
  • Add a one-line definition of 'agent-first' and a visual preview of the generated app.
  • Give every related product a short description and a clear role in the journey.
  • Introduce one primary CTA and show what happens after the first prompt.
  • Make the trust note readable and place it next to the output it refers to.
Deliverables
  • Annotated screenshots with the seven priority issues
  • Prioritised action plan
  • Copy suggestions for the hero and product labels
  • Written summary for the team

What the research showed

Early-bird pricing in batches creates urgency, and the 10-export free tier lets a designer prove value before paying. The metrics above are the product's own claims and have not been independently verified.

Sources, checked Oct 2026: ZipItFirefox add-onSocket.dev. Figures marked self-reported come from the product's own site.

The challenge

Designers who study or rebuild a site jump between DevTools, download folders, screenshots and Figma. Each switch costs time and loses context. ZipIt is designed to collapse that into one capture workflow.

Approach

  1. Map the workflow

    Listed every step a designer takes to pull assets from a live site and found where the tools break the flow.

  2. Define the modes

    Split the product into Download, Design and Inspect modes so each job has a clear home.

  3. Add the floating shelf

    Designed a temporary shelf that collects assets while browsing, so nothing is lost between pages.

  4. Brand and launch

    Built a warm, editorial identity in orange, near-black and cream, with a landing page, demo videos and pricing.

Design decisions
  • Designers think in jobs (grab a font, copy a color), not file types, so the modes follow the job.
  • A shelf that persists across pages removes the need to export after every capture.
  • Keeping data on the device is a trust requirement for client work, so it is stated up front.
What it does
  • Download Mode: a complete offline copy with HTML, CSS, images and fonts.
  • Design Mode: fonts, colors, gradients, SVGs, Lottie files and video.
  • Inspect Mode: styles, spacing and structure, with component export to Figma.
  • AI Rebuild: turns a capture into a prompt for ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor and v0.
  • Accessibility and SEO audit: WCAG and contrast checks plus SEO insights.
Deliverables
  • Product and interaction design
  • Brand identity and design tokens
  • Landing page and demo videos
  • Pricing: free tier with 10 exports, $25 lifetime

What users say

Praise centres on ease of use, speed and the background and object removal tools. Complaints centre on billing and cancellation clarity, slow support, object removal that sometimes cuts into the subject, and a new version that long-time users find less familiar. Those four themes shaped the design priorities: clear plan and billing states, visible undo, preview-before-apply for AI tools, and a consistent layout across platforms.

Sources, checked Oct 2026: inPixioTrustpilotCapterra. Figures marked self-reported come from the product's own site.

The challenge

Beginners want professional-looking photos without learning an editor. inPixio offers the same AI tools on the web, on a phone and on the desktop, so the design has to feel familiar on each surface while respecting how people use it.

Approach

  1. Find the core jobs

    Reduced the editor to the jobs people come for: remove a background, remove an object, swap a backdrop, adjust and export.

  2. Design once, adapt three times

    Built shared patterns and components, then adapted them to the web canvas, the touch screen and the offline desktop.

  3. Make AI feel safe

    Added clear before and after states, undo and previews so one-click actions feel reversible.

  4. Add motion

    Prototyped transitions that explain what the AI did, such as the cut-out lifting off its background.

Design decisions
  • Beginners judge an editor by the first result, so the main AI actions sit one tap from the image.
  • A consistent tool order across platforms lets people switch device without relearning.
  • Reversible actions raise confidence to try AI tools.
What it does
  • One-click background removal and transparent exports for product photos.
  • AI-generated backgrounds and sky swaps with live previews.
  • Magic eraser for object removal with a clear before and after.

What the research showed

Four plans cover three buyer types: try it free, pay monthly, or buy once. Ultimate is the best value for anyone who would use both web and desktop, so the pricing page leads with it. The user count is self-reported and unverified.

Sources, checked Oct 2026: ZipIt WebZipIt extension. Figures marked self-reported come from the product's own site.

The challenge

The extension needs an install, and some people just want assets from a URL right now. ZipIt Web removes the setup and adds a dashboard where captured assets are saved for later.

Approach

  1. Cut the setup

    Moved the core flow to a single URL field so a first capture takes seconds.

  2. Design the output

    Organised results into colors, fonts, media and tokens, each exportable as JSON, CSS variables or Figma-ready tokens.

  3. Add the cloud library

    Designed a dashboard to save, search and revisit captured assets.

  4. Package it

    Created pricing tiers that fit freelancers, teams and people who prefer a one-time purchase.

Design decisions
  • A URL field is the lowest-friction start for a tool like this.
  • People want the same assets in different forms, so every capture offers several export formats.
  • A saved library turns a one-off capture into a reference collection.
What it does
  • Full bundle: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, images, fonts and media as a ZIP.
  • Design tokens: colors, fonts, spacing and media exported as JSON, CSS variables or Figma-ready tokens.
  • DOM inspector: click an element to see styles and structure, with export to Figma layers or component code.
  • Accessibility (WCAG) and SEO audits, plus AI-ready output for regenerating components.
Deliverables
  • Web app UI and interaction design
  • Dashboard for saved assets
  • Brand and landing page
  • Pricing and plan structure
